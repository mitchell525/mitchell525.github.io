When I shipped this thing in May, the idea was that you load a magazine at home and then stop thinking about it. Six albums, chosen while you're standing in the kitchen, waiting for you when you get in the car.

That mostly worked. What kept breaking the illusion was the album that *wasn't* in a magazine. I'd be driving, want something specific, and have no way to get to it — the car could only see what I'd loaded. So I'd give up and put on whatever was in slot one, which is not the same as choosing.

1.3 is mostly about closing that gap.

## What I wanted

The pitch hasn't moved: the album is the unit. You pick a record and let it run. There are no playlists, no recommendations, no "because you listened to." The constraint is the whole point — a six-disc changer made you decide once, and deciding once is a better way to listen than deciding forty times.

But "decide once" only works if the thing you want is reachable. A changer in 1998 had a limitation I'd accidentally reproduced: if the disc wasn't in the magazine, it didn't exist. Except I have my entire library in my pocket, so that limitation was mine, not the format's.

## Why I built it

I've been a software engineer since 2012, and this one is a commute app in the most literal sense — I drive with it every day, and almost everything in this release is something that annoyed me between my driveway and somewhere else. That's the whole development process: notice a thing, write it down at a red light, fix it on a Saturday.

## Version 1.3

### A CarPlay Artists tab

There's a new tab in the car. Pick a letter, pick an artist, pick an album, and it plays — your whole library, not just what's loaded into magazines.

The letter step is the part I want to explain, because it looks like a design flourish and isn't. `CPListTemplate.maximumItemCount` caps how many items a template can hold, and it counts across *every section combined*, not per section. Go over it and CarPlay doesn't scroll or paginate — it silently drops the rest. A flat A-to-Z list of every artist in a decent-sized library sails past that cap and just quietly stops being complete, which is a worse failure than making you tap once more. Letters keep each screen well under the limit and, as a side effect, make the list navigable at a glance rather than by scrolling.

Artist rows show the artwork from that artist's first album. The letter rows deliberately don't show anything — resolving artwork for every letter meant fanning out across the library at the exact moment you're waiting for a screen.

Which brings up the bug I'm least proud of. On the device, tapping a letter had a beat of nothing before the list appeared. On a desk that's a shrug. In a car it isn't; the note I wrote to myself said "dangerous while driving," and I meant it. The app was resolving album artwork *before* pushing the screen. It now pushes the screen immediately and fills the artwork in as it arrives.

### Albums that fill the screen

The Discs tab used to show each magazine as a single row of covers, clipped at whatever fit. Apple Music's own CarPlay library view wraps its albums into a grid, and there was no good reason mine didn't.

This one cost me an afternoon and turned out to be one word. `CPListImageRowItem` was reworked in iOS 26, and the newer initializers take an `allowsMultipleLines` flag. My code was already calling the new initializer. It was passing `false`.

In fairness to me, the documentation for this is thin enough that I ended up reading `CPListImageRowItem.h` in the SDK directly rather than trusting what was written about it. In fairness to the three hours, the fix was a boolean.

Each magazine is now its own section with a chevron in the header, so tapping the title opens the full magazine as a list — the same shape as Apple Music's `Recently Added >`. There are caps in there too: CarPlay limits how many images a grid row can hold, and I cap albums per magazine grid at 24 and total grid artwork per build at 60, because the alternative is a beautiful screen that takes four seconds to appear.

### Artwork that stops going gray

The least interesting section and probably the one that mattered most. Album art could show up blank or permanently gray — in CarPlay, in the mini player, and on the lock screen — with the worst cases on Family Sharing albums and music synced locally rather than pulled from the catalog.

It's fixed, including the case where CarPlay would fall back to the "No Disc" placeholder for an album that was sitting right there. Cold launch is faster too, and artwork that's still loading now looks like it's loading instead of looking broken.

### The smaller things

- The design system was rebuilt on iOS 26's Liquid Glass. The Play button needed a contrast fix afterward, which is a sentence I expect to write again about translucent materials.
- An accessibility pass: VoiceOver across the app, a Dynamic Type audit of the icons, and a contrast fix that took the tertiary text from 4.20:1 to passing WCAG AA. It supports Dynamic Type and VoiceOver — I'm not going to claim more than that.
- The Live Activity and Now Playing widget are gone, removed a while back. The system's own lock screen and Dynamic Island already do that job, and mine was a worse copy of it.

### One more thing: the name

On the App Store it's now **CD Changer: Car Album Player**. Same app, same everything — "Digital" was doing no work, and nobody searches for it.

## What it still doesn't do

No single-song playback, no playlists, no shuffling your whole library. No iPad — CarPlay doesn't run there, and this is a CarPlay-companion app. No iCloud sync of your magazines between devices yet. And it can't play anything that isn't already in your Music library.
